# Juhi Agarwal / neXtHer — One-Page Client Brief

*The short version. For internal alignment and the first call. Detail lives in `marketing-strategy.md` and `reference-accounts-addendum.md`.*

---

## 1. The niche

**Indian mothers who want their own income, taught practical AI, by someone who understands why they haven't started.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | Married Indian women, roughly 28–45, most with children. Educated, often with a paused or abandoned career. Reads English, thinks in Hindi. Tier-1 and tier-2 cities. |
| **What they want** | Their own money. Earned from home, on their own hours, without a degree, a commute, or anyone's permission. |
| **Why they haven't** | Not the technology. It's "I'm not technical," "I'm too old," "it's too late," and the quiet fear of being judged for trying. |
| **The vehicle** | Practical AI — ChatGPT applied to real, sellable work. |
| **The real driver** | Autonomy and dignity. Not entrepreneurship. Not "hustle." |

### Why this is whitespace

It sits between three crowded categories, and none of them serve this woman:

| Category | Who it currently serves | Who it misses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AI education (India)** | Men, students, job-seekers. English, technical, career-shaped. | Her entirely |
| **Mom content (India)** | Parenting, lifestyle, product hauls | Her earning ambition |
| **Women & money (India)** | Investing and saving *money you already have* | Her — she needs to *earn* it first |

**Proof the niche works:** `@upgradingkatie` — 415K followers in the US doing almost exactly this ("Mom routines • money • glowup"). Proven concept, executed nowhere in India in Hinglish. **Unverified** — Instagram is blocked from our environment; needs a manual sweep.

She is **mid-pivot and hasn't announced it.** The Instagram account still sells the old business; the new one exists only in her head and in a WhatsApp message to us.

Her profile also carries four conflicting promises at once — burnout, mindset/healing, marriage repair, and (in the captions) a relationship call. A stranger cannot tell what she does.

**Assets she's sitting on:** 100+ women coached. Real fluency in the emotional lives of Indian married women. She's already on camera and consistent. And she can read the market — sending us Katie's profile proves she knows what "working" looks like.

**Her constraint:** she has never spoken on camera in 34 posts. Not once.

## 3. What she exactly wants

Three layers. They are not the same, and the gap between them is where the money is.

**Layer 1 — What she asked for (her words):**
> *"A digital production partner who can help me film, edit, publish, and grow high quality content as we build the brand together."*

She is shopping for **production**.

**Layer 2 — What the two reference accounts tell us she actually wants:**
- Sending `@upgradingkatie` = *"this is the business I want"* — mom + AI + money, 415K, self-shot, monetised
- Sending `@melrobbins` = *"this is the stature I want"* — author, podcast, tour, named idea

Read together, that's a clearer brief than anything in her bio. **She has told us the destination even though her own account still points somewhere else.**

**Layer 3 — What she needs before production matters:**
1. **A positioning decision** — is `@juhiagarwal.coach` becoming neXtHer, or does neXtHer get its own account? Nothing starts until this is settled.
2. **A named method** — the Mel Robbins mechanic. Not a slogan; a procedure with a name that a woman can describe to her sister in one sentence.
3. **A narrative spine** — Katie's engine is a public experiment with a clock on it, not tips. Every post an episode.
4. **Volume** — Katie has 369 posts. Juhi has 34. This is the gap, and it's the most boring and most important item on the list.
5. **A funnel** — pinned triptych, highlights, WhatsApp list, a store link. She has none of these.

Production is **item six**. It is real work, but it is not the bottleneck, and filming beautifully against unresolved positioning just produces expensive noise.

## 4. What we can do for her

### The honest framing

If we pitch as a film crew, we get commodity rates and we get blamed when growth is slow — because the things that actually drive growth won't be in our scope. If we pitch as the system that produces and distributes and converts, we own the outcome and it's a better business for both sides.

**We're not selling filming. We're selling: she posts 5× a week in her own voice instead of twice, and there's a funnel underneath it.**

Note this also makes us *cheaper* to deliver — the evidence says selfie-camera beats cinematic in this niche, so the shoots we drop are the expensive ones.

### Scope

| Phase | What | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Strategy sprint** *(paid, fixed fee)* | Settle pivot vs. second brand. Build the named method and the experiment format. Rewrite bio. Voice guide from a 90-min interview. Competitive sweep. Review her Insights. | 2 weeks |
| **2. Content engine** *(monthly retainer)* | 5 reels/week + 1 YouTube/week from her self-shot footage. Editing, captions in her voice, covers, publishing, scheduling. Pinned triptych and highlights. Weekly performance readout. | Ongoing |
| **3. Funnel & monetisation** *(retainer or project)* | WhatsApp list, DM keyword automation, store setup, launch support for the ₹999 workshop and then the cohort. | From week 4 |

### What stays hers — and this is a condition, not a preference

DMs, comments, sales calls, teaching. **20 minutes a day in her own inbox.** The funnel dies without her in it, and no agency can fake being the coach.

### What she gives us

Self-shot footage on her phone (we supply the setup and a two-page how-to), roughly 30 minutes a week. Willingness to be on camera, talking, in Hinglish. Willingness to be visibly mid-experiment rather than a finished authority.

## 5. Before we quote

| Question | Why it decides the scope |
| --- | --- |
| Pivot or second brand? | Doubles or halves the content load |
| What is "Trusted by 100+ Women"? | Paid clients or free sessions? Changes whether she has proof or a claim |
| What can she genuinely teach in AI? | Everything assumes real depth. If it's thin, the whole plan changes |
| Budget and runway | How long before this must pay for itself |
| Will she be on camera, talking, in Hinglish? | If no, most of this doesn't work and we design something else |
| Katie's aesthetic or Mel's? | If she wants Mel's polish at 1.2k followers, settle it now — it won't grow the account |

### Risk of taking this on

She's early-stage, small following, unvalidated offer, and probably a modest budget. The ₹999 workshop in week 5 is the cheapest possible test of whether her audience will pay for AI training — **run it before anyone builds a cohort.** Worth structuring the engagement so that test happens early and either side can walk if it fails.

The encouraging signal: she sent references. Clients who can articulate the destination are far easier to steer than clients who can't.

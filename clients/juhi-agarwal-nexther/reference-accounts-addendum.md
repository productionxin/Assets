## 2. Mel Robbins is the wrong template — for now

Mel's grid is podcast clips with credentialed guests (Gabor Maté, a Yale psychologist, an Olympic gold medalist), quote cards on solid colour with an "xo Mel" signature, and a TIME Women of the Year cover.

None of that is a growth engine. It is **distribution for an existing media empire** — a top-tier podcast, the #1 selling book of 2025, a protein brand, a 2026 tour. The Instagram account is downstream of the authority, not the cause of it.

Copying it at 1,209 followers produces nothing. Quote cards work because they carry Mel's name; from an unknown account they are wallpaper. Expert-guest clips work because experts say yes to Mel. **You cannot borrow authority you do not have.**

**But one thing from Mel transfers, and it may be the single most valuable idea in this document:**

> Mel's entire career rests on **naming a mechanism**. The 5 Second Rule. The Let Them Theory. Not advice — a named, ownable, repeatable thing that people can say to each other when she isn't in the room.

neXtHer needs its own named method. Not a slogan — a *procedure* with a name, that a woman can describe to her sister in one sentence. Katie has an informal version ("I described my dream life to ChatGPT and it gives me a daily to-do list"). Juhi should have a deliberate one. **This is worth a dedicated working session, and it should happen before the content rebuild, not after** — because once it exists, every piece of content becomes an episode of it rather than a standalone tip.

**Correct framing: Katie is the blueprint. Mel is the destination. The distance between them is a named idea and ten years.**

## 3. What Katie actually does — the mechanics worth stealing

### 3.1 It's a documented experiment, not a course

Her viral caption:

> *"I made up a weird little system for changing your life and it's working... I described my dream life to chatgpt and now every day it gives me a to do list to help me get there. It's been **86 days** of small boring tasks combined with a few bigger and bolder moves and a lot has happened. **If I can do this in less than 3 months, what can we do in a year?!**"*

She is not teaching AI. She is **running a public experiment on herself with a clock on it**, and letting people watch. That is the engine, and it is a completely different content physics from "5 tips."

Why it works, and why it matters more than any format change:
- **A serialised story compounds.** Every post is an episode. Tips do not compound; episodes do. Followers come back because they want to know what happened, not because they need another tip.
- **It is inherently honest.** She reports what didn't work. Nobody can accuse her of selling a dream because she is visibly still inside the experiment.
- **It removes the authority problem entirely.** She does not need to be an expert. She is a subject. That is a position anyone can occupy on day one — including someone at 1,209 followers.
- **It has a built-in ending and sequel.** "What can we do in a year?"

**This is the biggest gap in my first draft.** I gave content pillars but no spine. Pillars are a filing system; the experiment is the story. Juhi needs her own — something with a start date, a visible clock, a rule she has to follow, and honest reporting. Her version has to be Indian and income-shaped rather than glow-up-shaped. Design it in the first working session.

### 3.2 The comment engine

Under the viral post, the same comment appears dozens of times:

> "Prompt please 🙏" · "Prompt please ❤️" · "Prompt please!! 🔥"

And every one has a reply from her.

This is the DM funnel in its highest form. She never says "comment PROMPT below." **She builds content where asking for the asset is the natural human reaction.** The audience runs the funnel themselves.

The mechanic underneath: *show the result, withhold the method.* She demonstrates that something worked; the prompt that produced it is the obvious next question.

That is directly copyable and costs nothing. Juhi's current captions do the opposite — they give the full five-point list in the caption and then ask for a booking. Nothing is left to want.

### 3.3 Profile as a funnel

Katie's profile does three jobs Juhi's does not:

| | Katie | Juhi |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pinned posts | **START HERE / HOW IT WORKS / WHAT'S CHANGED** — a deliberate onboarding triptych for new visitors | none |
| Highlights | Free Prompts · Start Here · Dream Life · How it works · Q&A · challenges — a navigable funnel | one, "daily motiva…" |
| Store | `stan.store` — self-serve, low-ticket, always open | none |
| Following | 1,155 | **5** |

The pinned triptych is cheap and high-leverage: three videos that answer *what is this, how does it work, does it work.* Juhi should have these live within two weeks. The India equivalent of stan.store is Topmate, Graphy, Instamojo or a Razorpay page — worth an hour of comparison, not more.

### 3.4 Bio construction

- **Katie:** "Building my dream life one AI generated to do list at a time. — Mom routines • money • glowup"
- **Juhi:** "Helping Ambitious Women Overcome Burnout | Mindset • Healing • Confidence"

Same shape, opposite content. Katie's three words are **concrete nouns** — routines, money, glowup. Juhi's are **abstractions** — mindset, healing, confidence. Abstractions do not tell a stranger what they will get, and they do not differentiate, because every coach on Instagram claims all three.

Also note Katie's first line is about **herself doing something**, not about helping you. It is an invitation to watch, which is a far lower bar than an offer to fix you.

### 3.5 Hashtags

Katie: `#chatgptprompts #momglowup #selfimprovementjourney #momlife #chatgptglowup` — five, niche, no keyword dump.

This confirms the recommendation in §2 of the main strategy. Juhi's pre-hashtag keyword blocks should be deleted outright.

## 4. ⚠️ Revision: production values

**This changes the scope I proposed in §9 of the main strategy.**

Katie's grid is almost entirely **selfie-camera video** — handheld, in her kitchen, in her car, no lighting rig, no crew. One post is literally her saying *"Okay I just posted a vlog and I'm so intimidated by vlogs."* She shows the fear on camera.

That is not an accident of budget. In this niche, **low production is a trust signal.** She looks like the woman watching her. A polished, colour-graded, cinematically-lit video would read as an ad and convert worse.

My first draft leaned toward batch shoot days and high production values. **On this evidence that is partly wrong**, and I'd rather correct it now than sell a service that hurts the outcome.

**What this means for the engagement — and it makes it a stronger offer, not a weaker one:**

| Lower priority than I said | Higher priority than I said |
| --- | --- |
| Cinematography, lighting, location shoots | **Volume** — Katie has 369 posts, Juhi has 34. This is the gap. |
| Colour grading, polish | **Fast turnaround** — captions, cuts, publishing, 5×/week without fail |
| Scripted studio setups | **The narrative spine** — designing and running the experiment format |
| | **The comment engine** — replying to every comment, routing to WhatsApp |
| | **Repurposing** — one self-shot clip into Reels, Shorts, YouTube, Threads |
| | **Coaching her on-camera confidence**, not replacing her with production |

The b-roll shoots (walking down stairs, strawberry fields) should largely stop. They are the most expensive thing on the account and the least effective.

**The honest version of the pitch:** we are not a film crew. We are the system that lets her post 5×/week instead of twice, in her own voice, on her own phone, and keeps the funnel running underneath it. That is a better business for both sides — cheaper to deliver, harder to churn out of, and it is what the evidence says actually works.

## 5. ⚠️ Revision: don't discard the relationship expertise

In the main strategy I said to burn the relationship niche. **Refining that: burn the format, keep the insight.**

Here is what Katie cannot do and Juhi can. Katie is an American mother in a suburban house talking about glow-up and dream lives. Direct imitation in India fails — the texture is wrong. The Indian version of this audience has a different reality: in-laws, joint families, permission, judgement about earning, the specific shame of asking a husband for money.

**Juhi has spent 34 posts and 100+ clients getting fluent in exactly that emotional reality.** No AI educator in India has it. That is the moat.

So the mindset work stops being the product and becomes the **differentiator**:

> *"I know exactly why you won't start. I've spent years talking to women who felt the same way — and it was never the technology."*

That is a sentence Katie cannot say and no Indian AI educator can say either. It sits at the intersection, and it is the reason her version isn't a copy.

Practically: keep mindset at roughly 15% of output as planned, but reframe it from *healing* to *the specific blocks that stop an Indian woman starting.* And the bridge video from §4 of the main strategy is now more important, not less — it converts her past into her credential.

## 6. One more structural note

Katie appears to run a second account, `@itskatie.ai` — "Katie | AI Creator Coach." I could not verify with confidence that it is the same person (Instagram is blocked and there are several Katies in this space, plus an unrelated enterprise product called "Katie AI"), so treat this as unconfirmed.

But the *pattern* is worth knowing, because it answers Juhi's pivot-vs-second-brand question from a different angle: the audience account teaches the thing, and a separate account teaches **how to do what the first account did**. It is a common and lucrative second act — but it is a year-two move. Raising it now would be a distraction. Flagging it so it isn't a surprise later.

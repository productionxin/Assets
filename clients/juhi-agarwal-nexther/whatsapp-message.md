# Message + call plan

**Where things stand:** last chat 30 July. You cancelled the call (family emergency), told her you went through her profile and references, said you'll share ideas once back. She replied *"Ok. Please take care."* That was 8 days ago.

**Tomorrow's call = you listening.** Not pitching.

---

## Don't send the deck yet

Keep the proposal in your drive for now. Two reasons:

1. You still don't fully understand her business, because she hasn't explained it yet. Anything you send now is a guess.
2. The deck is blunt. It says her account is advertising four different businesses and that she has never spoken on camera. Coming from someone who cancelled on her, before she has been heard, that will feel like an insult. After she talks for 40 minutes, the same points become useful.

Nothing is wasted. The deck just goes second.

## The call — 45 minutes

**You should talk for only 10 of those minutes.** Keep this open on your laptop. Tell her you are taking notes, she will not mind.

### Start (2 min)

> "Before I show you anything, I want to understand the business properly. Can I ask you some questions?"

### Her story (8 min)

> **"Tell me about neXtHer. How did you start thinking about it?"**

Then keep quiet. Let her talk. Only push with:
- *"What made you start this?"*
- *"If it goes well, where do you see it in 2 years?"*

**Notice:** is she really committed to this, or is it just an idea she is excited about right now? Her energy will tell you more than her words.

### What is ready today (5 min)

- **"What all is ready right now? Website, any course, anything?"**
- **"Has anyone paid you for neXtHer related work till now?"**
- **"Your bio says trusted by 100+ women. Can you tell me about them? Paid clients or free sessions?"**

**Notice:** does she have proof, or only a claim? This decides whether she can sell something in month two, or has to build it first.

### Who is it for (5 min)

- **"Think of one woman who is your ideal client. Can you describe her? What is her day like, what is she stuck on?"**
- **"Do you actually know someone like this, someone you have coached?"**

**Notice:** does she describe a real person or just "women, 25 to 40"? A real person means she knows her market.

### The product and money (7 min)

- **"What will she buy from you, and what price are you thinking?"**
- **"When you say AI, what exactly will you teach her? Can you walk me through it?"**
- **"What do you use AI for in your own work daily?"**

**Notice:** can she tell you what a student will be able to *do* after? If she cannot, that is the biggest gap in the whole plan. Better you find out now than after quoting.

### The main question (5 min)

Ask this softly, like you are curious. Not like you are pointing out a mistake.

> **"One thing I want to understand. Most of your posts are about relationships and marriage. But what you told me is about AI and income. How are these two connected for you?"**

**This one question is the whole call.** Either she explains a connection you did not see, and you learn something. Or she stops and sees the gap herself.

Her realising it in her own words is 10 times better than you telling her.

**Don't fill the silence.** Let her think.

### What she wants from you (7 min)

- **"When you say production partner, what exactly do you picture me doing?"**
- **"Which part you want to take off your plate first?"**
- **"Have you worked with anyone for content before? How was it?"**
- **"How much time can you give for this every week?"**

**Notice:** does she want a camera person or a partner? Her answer decides what you sell her, and whether this is even worth taking.

### Practical (3 min)

- **"What timeline do you have in mind?"**
- **"Have you thought about a budget for this?"**

Ask it normally. If you feel shy about money now, it will be harder later.

### Close (3 min)

> "This was really helpful. Give me a few days, I will put together how I would approach it, based on what you told me. Can we talk again on **[day]**?"

**Book the next call before hanging up.** That is the only thing tomorrow needs to achieve.

## Simple rules for the call

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Let silence sit. She will fill it with the useful part | Jump in to help |
| Say "tell me more about that" at least 3 times | Pitch. Not even once |
| Write her exact words. Later they become her content voice | Correct her or argue about her account |

**If she asks the price:**

> "Let me understand everything properly first, then I will come back with proper numbers. I don't want to quote you for the wrong thing."

Then move on.

---

## After the call

Update the deck with what she actually said, and present it on call two. Most of it will stay same. The difference is it will be built on her words, and you will be able to explain every slide, because by then you will actually understand the business.

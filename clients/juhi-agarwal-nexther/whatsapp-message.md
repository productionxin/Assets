# WhatsApp — sending the proposal

*Copy-paste ready. Send as separate messages, not one block — WhatsApp rewards short bursts, and a wall of text on a phone gets skimmed.*

---

## The rule this is built on

**Do not let her open the deck cold.** If she hits ₹65,000 on slide 10 without having read the diagnosis first, she bounces on price alone. The message's only job is to make her read slides 3 and 7 — then the deck does the selling.

**And do not quote a number over text.** The ask is a call, not a decision.

---

## Primary sequence

### Message 1 — the hook

> Hi Juhi — thanks for sending Katie's and Mel's profiles. That was more useful than you probably intended.
>
> I went through your account properly before replying, along with both references and what's happening in this space in India. Ended up doing a lot more work than I planned to.

### Message 2 — the finding

> Short version: @upgradingkatie is doing almost exactly what you described neXtHer to be. 415K followers. So the concept is proven — and as far as I can tell, nobody is doing it in India in Hinglish.
>
> But I also found something in your current account that I think is quietly costing you. It's not what you'd expect, and it's cheap to fix right now in a way it won't be in a year.

### Message 3 — the deck

> 📎 *[attach neXtHer-Proposal.pdf]*
>
> Put it all in here.

### Message 4 — the ask

> Don't feel you need to read all of it. Slides 3 and 7 are the two that matter.
>
> If the diagnosis lands, can I have 45 minutes this week? And if you think I've read it wrong, tell me — there's no point talking about anything else until we agree on the problem.

---

## Voice note version *(recommended — send instead of messages 1 and 2)*

Voice notes outperform text for this in India, and this pitch is largely about whether she trusts your judgement. Hearing you think is worth more than reading you.

**Keep it 40–50 seconds. Don't script it word for word — talk from these beats:**

1. Thanks for sending Katie and Mel — genuinely useful, and they told me more than the brief did
2. I went and did the homework before replying, because I didn't want to just quote you for filming
3. Katie is at 415K doing almost exactly what you described neXtHer to be — so the idea is proven, and nobody's doing it here
4. But there's something in your current account that I think is working against you, and I'd rather show you than explain it over text
5. Sending a short deck — slides 3 and 7 are the ones. 45 minutes this week?

Then send the PDF right after.

## If she replies "how much?" before the call

Don't dodge it — dodging reads as expensive. But don't let it become the conversation.

> It's in the deck — there are three ways to run it, starting at ₹35,000/month, and one option where I take a lower fee and a share of what the programmes earn instead.
>
> But I'd rather not talk numbers before you've told me whether the diagnosis on slide 3 is right. If that's wrong, none of the pricing matters.

## Follow-ups

**Day 3 — no reply**

> Hi Juhi, no rush on the deck. One thing I'd genuinely like to know either way: has anyone ever paid you for AI training, or would neXtHer be starting from zero on that?
>
> Changes what I'd recommend quite a bit.

*A real question, not a nudge — it's the biggest open risk in the plan, and it's much easier to answer than "did you read it?"*

**Day 7 — still nothing**

> Last one from me — I'll leave it with you.
>
> If the timing's not right, no problem at all. If it's the pivot itself that's giving you pause, that's the part worth a conversation, and I'm happy to have it with no proposal attached.

*Then stop. Two follow-ups is the limit.*

---

## Don't

| ✗ | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Send the deck with no framing | She meets the price before the diagnosis |
| Quote a number in the first message | Turns it into a price comparison against videographers |
| Say "let me know your thoughts" | Weak close, invites silence |
| Send a fifth message after day 7 | Chasing kills the deal you might still get later |
| Send after 9pm | It's a business proposal; make it look like one |

---

## Timing

Send **10am–1pm on a weekday.** Her own posting pattern suggests she's active in the morning. Avoid Friday evening — it sits unread until Monday and goes cold.

# Red Rose Mart — Messaging Framework

**Prepared:** 30 July 2026 · **For:** first meeting with the Red Rose team
**Built on:** `research/red-rose-mart-positioning.md` · **Context:** `.agents/brand-context.md`

**Working assumption:** the positioning emphasis shift is accepted — *apna* (ours) leads,
*sasta* (cheapest) becomes proof. §09 carries a fallback if the client insists price stays hero.

> **Two flags before the content.**
>
> **1. Language validation is required, not optional.** The Urdu/Dakhni lines below are
> strategically directed and idiomatically drafted, but they must be validated and
> refined by a native Dakhni-speaking copywriter before any public use. Hyderabadi
> Dakhni has its own register and humour that does not survive translation-by-committee,
> and getting it slightly wrong is worse than writing in English — it reads as an
> outsider imitating the audience. **Treat every line here as direction, not final copy.**
>
> **2. Their own transliteration is already inconsistent.** The Instagram bio reads
> *"Apne Shaher Mein Sab Se Sasta"*; trade press renders it *"Apne shahar mein sabse
> sasta"*. Spelling, spacing and capitalisation all vary. A house transliteration
> standard is a small, cheap fix that should be settled in this workstream — I use
> **"Shaher"** and **"sabse"** below as the apparent house forms.

---

## 01 — CORE MESSAGE

The strategic anchor. Not a tagline — the thing every piece of communication must ultimately serve.

> **Red Rose Mart helps families in Hyderabad's own neighbourhoods do the entire household
> shop affordably and close to home, by pairing supermarket range and everyday-low prices
> with a store built around how this city actually eats, cooks and celebrates.**

Three words are doing load-bearing work: **entire** (the full shop, not the top-up),
**close to home** (the incumbency advantage), **this city** (the moat).

---

## 02 — VALUE PROPOSITION

> Red Rose Mart is the supermarket that belongs to Hyderabad's own neighbourhoods. Families
> get the whole month's shop under one roof — fresh produce, halal meat, groceries,
> sweet-meats, a live Pista House bakery, pharmacy and household goods — at prices that
> hold every single day of the year, not just on offer weekends.
>
> The difference is not only what it costs. It is that the store is stocked, staffed and
> run by people from here, who know which brands this neighbourhood buys, which festivals
> fill the trolley, and what a family shop actually looks like. National chains price this
> catchment from a spreadsheet in another city. Red Rose has been part of Hyderabad for 45 years.

---

## 03 — TAGLINE OPTIONS

Including the incumbent for comparison. All options deliberately retain *apna* — the equity
is real and there is no reason to spend it.

| # | Tagline | Style | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0** | *Apne Shaher Mein Sabse Sasta* **(current)** | Functional | Real recall, but leads with the one claim DMart owns and Red Rose cannot defend. Also an unqualified superlative — a legal exposure as well as a strategic one. **Retire as hero; the DNA survives in the options below.** |
| **1** ★ | **Apna Mart. Har Roz Sasta.** | Functional–emotional | **Recommended.** The tagline itself performs the hierarchy: belonging in the first clause, price demoted to the second. It keeps *both* words the brand already owns, so nothing is thrown away — the argument changes without the recall changing. And "har roz" (every day) converts the price claim from a superlative into a *guarantee*, which is both more defensible and more true to the 6% EDLP model. |
| 2 | **Apna Shaher. Apna Mart.** | Emotional | Purest expression of the position, and the most memorable as a chant or jingle. Loses the price proof entirely, which is a real cost in a value category — works best as a campaign or brand-film line sitting *above* option 1. |
| 3 | **Apne Shaher Ka Apna Mart** | Emotional | Closest to the incumbent structurally, so the softest landing internally. Slightly long, and the repeated *apna* is either pleasing or clumsy depending on delivery — a native ear should arbitrate. |
| 4 | **Your City. Your Mart.** | Emotional | The English-surface rendering for LinkedIn, corporate, trade press and supplier decks. Not a translation of option 1 but its counterpart — the register shifts, the idea holds. **Replaces "Where Quality Meets Affordability", which should be retired outright.** |
| 5 | **Poora Ghar, Ek Chhat Ke Neeche** *(The whole household, under one roof)* | Functional | Attacks the kirana on range rather than the chains on price, which is the fight Red Rose actually wins. Strong as a *category* line for the flagship launch, where 75,000 sq ft is the story — weaker as an enduring brand line. |

**Recommendation: option 1, "Apna Mart. Har Roz Sasta."** — with option 2 held as the
emotional campaign line and option 4 as the English corporate surface. That combination
gives the brand one hero, one anthem and one boardroom register, all arguing the same thing.

## 04 — MESSAGING HIERARCHY

### Level 1 — Brand Headline

> **Apna Mart. Har Roz Sasta.**
> *(English surface: Your city's own mart. Everyday low prices.)*

### Level 2 — Supporting Statement

> The whole family's shop — fresh, halal, groceries, bakery and household — under one roof,
> at prices that hold every day of the year. From a name that has been part of Hyderabad
> for 45 years.

### Level 3 — Key Messages (four pillars)

**Pillar 1 — "Apna hai" — This one is ours**
The store belongs to the neighbourhood it stands in. Same city, same streets, same
festivals, same families. Not a national chain that opened a branch here and priced it
from somewhere else.
→ **Proof:** The Red Rose Group has operated in Hyderabad since 1980 — 45 years — with five
stores concentrated in the catchments it actually serves. *[Origin story needs reconciling before public use.]*

**Pillar 2 — The whole shop, one roof**
Fresh fruit and vegetables, a halal meat counter, groceries, sweet-meats, a live bakery,
pharmacy, cosmetics, household goods and a coffee shop. The full monthly shop finished in
one trip, without a second stop.
→ **Proof:** Store formats from 26,000 sq ft up to a 75,000 sq ft flagship, with a live
Pista House bakery where the bakers work in front of you.

**Pillar 3 — Prices that hold, every day**
Not an offer weekend. Not a festival sale. A standing minimum discount across the range,
every day of the year — so the family budget works the same in the third week of the
month as the first.
→ **Proof:** Minimum 6% off across products, 365 days a year. *[Must be substantiated and
legally reviewed before this becomes a public claim — see §07.]*

**Pillar 4 — Stocked for how we eat**
The range is built around this community's kitchen, not a national planogram — the brands
this neighbourhood actually buys, the cuts it actually cooks, and the volumes Ramzan,
Sankranti and Dussehra actually demand.
→ **Proof:** Halal meat and sweet-meat counters as standing departments; festival-scale
range planning; title sponsorship of Daawat Dastarkhan. *[Sponsorship to be confirmed.]*

### Level 4 — Proof Point Bank

Verified or client-sourced, for use across all channels. **[V]** = needs verification before public use.

1. **45 years in Hyderabad** — group trading since 1980. **[V — reconcile origin stories]**
2. **Minimum 6% off, 365 days a year**, across the range. **[V — substantiate + legal read]**
3. **Live Pista House bakery in-store**, bakers working in public view. **[V — confirm exclusivity and per-store rollout]**
4. **~4.2★ across 1,660+ Google reviews** — fresh produce, layout and affordability the recurring praise.
5. **Five stores, ~80,000 sq ft**, plus a **75,000 sq ft flagship** opening. **[V]**
6. **~1.5 lakh transactions a month** — "1.5 lakh families every month" is the human rendering. **[V]**
7. **Open 8:00 AM – 11:00 PM**, home delivery on 9030303752.
8. **Full-service departments** under one roof: fresh, halal meat, sweet-meats, bakery, pharmacy, cosmetics, coffee shop.
9. **Festival-scale range planning** across Ramzan, Sankranti and Dussehra.

## 05 — AUDIENCE-SPECIFIC MESSAGING

### Primary — the household shop decision-maker (old city and adjacent quarters)
The person planning and executing the weekly or monthly family shop. Budget-disciplined,
brand-loyal within categories, culturally rooted, Dakhni/Urdu-first with Hindi and English fluency.

- **What they care most about:** total bill for a full trolley · not having to make a second stop · freshness they can judge with their own eyes · the specific brands they always buy being in stock · not being made to feel talked down to
- **How to lead:** lead with *apna* and the completeness of the shop, then land price as reassurance. Dakhni/Urdu-first, warm, family-scale, second person. Speak about the trolley and the month, not about "retail" or "value propositions."
- **Proof points that land hardest:** 6% every day (budget certainty) · the whole shop under one roof · halal meat and sweet-meats as standing counters · Pista House · 45 years
- **Avoid:** English-first corporate register · unqualified superlatives they can disprove at the till · anything implying the store is a treat or an upgrade rather than their normal · urgency and scarcity language borrowed from q-commerce

### Secondary A — young digital Hyderabad (the 29.7K Instagram audience)
A materially different audience from the primary, currently served by the same channel — worth
naming as a distinct target rather than a bonus.

- **What they care most about:** cultural pride and shareability · the brand doing something genuinely notable · food · humour and Hyderabadi in-jokes
- **How to lead:** lead with the city, not the trolley. *Apna shaher* as identity, not as a grocery argument. Bilingual, playful, visually confident — the existing F1 and festival content already has the right instinct.
- **Proof points that land hardest:** Pista House · the flagship scale · Daawat Dastarkhan · anything with genuine Hyderabadi specificity
- **Avoid:** price-led posts as the default (this audience does not do the household shop) · treating them as a lower-funnel channel · **claiming sponsorships or partnerships that cannot be substantiated** — this is the audience most likely to notice

### Secondary B — talent, suppliers and trade (LinkedIn, corporate, trade press)
Currently the weakest surface: 80 followers against 29.7K, for a business with 201–500
employees hiring toward a 100-store ambition.

- **What they care most about:** growth trajectory and whether this is a serious operator · leadership credibility · whether the expansion story is real
- **How to lead:** lead with the growth record and the operating discipline, in English. *"Your City. Your Mart."* as the surface line. The 25-year-old-CEO story is a genuine asset here.
- **Proof points that land hardest:** ₹75cr → ₹125cr **[V]** · five stores in three years with a 75,000 sq ft flagship pending · 1.5 lakh monthly transactions · ₹1,950 per sq ft per day **[V]** · 45-year group heritage across six divisions
- **Avoid:** *"Where Quality Meets Affordability"* and every phrase like it · consumer price messaging in a professional context · inflated headcount claims that contradict the visible employee count

## 06 — MESSAGING BY CHANNEL

Core message constant; register, language and length flex.

| Channel | Headline approach | Language | Tone | Length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Website hero** | Hero tagline + the whole-shop promise + store locator as the primary action | Urdu/Hindi transliterated headline, English body | Warm, plain, practical | Short headline, medium support |
| **Instagram bio** | Tagline, branches, timings, delivery number — it already does this well; keep the structure, swap the hero line | Transliterated Urdu + English | Direct, useful | Very short |
| **Instagram content** | City pride and food first; offers as a distinct recurring format, not the default | Bilingual, Dakhni-inflected | Playful, confident, local | Very short |
| **In-store & signage** | Pillar 2 and 3 language at the aisle; pillar 1 at the entrance | Urdu-first, Hindi, English tertiary | Warm, unambiguous | Very short |
| **Offer leaflets / print drops** | Price-led — the one place price legitimately leads, because intent is already established | Urdu/Hindi primary | Plain, specific, no hype | Short |
| **WhatsApp / delivery** | Utility first, relationship second; name the customer | Urdu/Hindi | Personal, brief | Very short |
| **Paid social & local ads** | Whole-shop completeness for cold audiences; price only for warm retargeting | Bilingual | Confident | Very short |
| **LinkedIn / trade / supplier decks** | Growth record and operating discipline; *"Your City. Your Mart."* | English | Measured, credible | Medium |
| **PR / flagship launch** | Scale and the city story together — 75,000 sq ft *for Hyderabad* | English + Urdu quotes | Ambitious, rooted | Medium |

**Language architecture, stated plainly:** **Urdu/Dakhni carries the belonging.** **Hindi
extends reach** across the wider catchment. **English carries the credibility** for trade,
talent and press. The current failure is not that three languages are in use — it is that
each is saying something *different*. One idea, three registers.

## 07 — THINGS NOT TO SAY

**Retire immediately**
- *"Where Quality Meets Affordability"* — a non-position; works verbatim for every grocer alive.
- **Unqualified "sabse sasta" / "cheapest" as a standalone hero claim.** Undefendable against DMart's cost structure and legally exposed as a superlative. It survives only in the demoted, guaranteed form: *"har roz sasta."*
- **"One-stop shop", "world-class", "unbeatable deals", "your trusted partner"** — category wallpaper. Note the current Instagram creative already uses *"Unbeatable Deals"*; it should go.

**Do not claim until verified**
- The **6% figure** — specific and therefore checkable, which is what makes it powerful and what makes it a liability if it is not literally true across the range.
- **Red Bull / F1** as a sponsorship. The creative reads like a stocked-brand relationship dressed as an F1 tie-up. Overclaiming this is a reputational risk with exactly the audience most equipped to check.
- **Daawat Dastarkhan title sponsorship** — appears only on owned channels.
- **Pista House exclusivity** and whether it exists at every store.
- **Any revenue, footfall or headcount figure** in public material until audited. The LinkedIn "201–500 employees" against 11 visible profiles is already an inconsistency someone could notice.

**Do not say, on strategy grounds**
- Anything **promising speed or delivery times.** Red Rose is not in the convenience fight; setting a speed expectation invites a comparison it loses.
- **Premium, luxury, curated, upscale.** Affordability is a promise to keep, not a phase to outgrow.
- **Scarcity and urgency language** ("hurry", "last chance", "only today") as a default. It contradicts *"prices that hold every day"* — the entire point of EDLP is that you do not need to rush.
- **Religiously exclusive framing at brand level.** Cultural specificity belongs in range, festival calendar and store-level communication. The brand-level idea is *apna shaher* — civic Hyderabadi — because that is the version that travels to Shadnagar and beyond.

**Do not say, until operations catch up**
- **Any trust-led or "we look after you" message** while the *"deceptive gift-hamper calls"* issue is unresolved. A belonging position trades on trust; running trust copy into an active trust problem amplifies the damage.
- **Anything about a fast, easy, effortless trip** while checkout queues remain the top recurring complaint. Do not market a fix that has not shipped.

## 08 — MESSAGING RED FLAGS IN THE CURRENT WORK

1. **Two heroes, two languages, two arguments** — Urdu price message on Instagram, English quality message on LinkedIn, no hierarchy. This framework resolves it.
2. **The revenue-driving audience is unaddressed.** Neither existing voice is written for the household shop decision-maker: Instagram speaks to young digital Hyderabad, LinkedIn to nobody in particular.
3. **Price is doing all the work in a category where price cannot differentiate.** Every competitor discounts. Only Red Rose can be *apna*.
4. **Proof points exist but are not deployed as proof.** Pista House, 45 years, and the department mix are shown as content and never argued as reasons to believe.
5. **Inconsistent transliteration** of the brand's own core line across its own channels.

---

## 09 — FALLBACK: IF THE CLIENT INSISTS PRICE STAYS HERO

Do not abandon the framework — invert two levels and hold the rest.

- **Hero becomes** *"Apne Shaher Mein Sabse Sasta"* retained, but with the superlative qualified to survive scrutiny: **"Sabse sasta — har roz, poore saal"** *(cheapest — every day, all year)*. The qualifier converts an unwinnable absolute claim into a defensible EDLP guarantee, which is the minimum legal and strategic fix even if nothing else changes.
- **Pillar order becomes** 3 → 2 → 4 → 1. Price leads, range second, cultural range third, belonging as the closing warmth.
- **What must not change:** *apna* stays in the tagline and in every pillar's language, "Where Quality Meets Affordability" still retires, verification gates still apply, and the four "do not say" categories in §07 all still hold.
- **The cost, stated for the record:** a price-hero strategy commits Red Rose to defending a claim against a competitor with a structural cost advantage, and concedes the only territory on the map that scale cannot buy. It is survivable. It is not the recommendation.
